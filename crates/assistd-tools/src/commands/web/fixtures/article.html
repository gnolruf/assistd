Building a Ferris Wheel

The first Ferris wheel was built for the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago, and it was meant to rival the Eiffel Tower, which had opened in Paris four years earlier.

Its designer, George Washington Gale Ferris Jr., was a bridge builder by trade, so the wheel borrowed heavily from bridge engineering: steel trusses, tension spokes, and a single enormous axle.

The axle

The axle weighed more than seventy tonnes, was forged in one piece, and was at the time the largest hollow forging in the world, which made lifting it onto its towers a project of its own.

Each of the thirty-six cars held sixty passengers, and a full revolution, with stops to load and unload, took about twenty minutes.
